# Presentation script: road financing in Brazil, described
Meeting with Vivien, 22 October 2026. Page: descriptive_statistics_road_financing.html. Metadata: metadata_descriptive_statistics.xlsx (one sheet per chart). Time: about 30 minutes plus discussion.

## Opening (2 minutes)
- What this is: the descriptive layer of the thesis, built so that I can start coding governance parameters. Every number comes from the bases we have been consolidating; nothing is typed by hand; stages are never added; what cannot be measured is said.
- The question behind the page: there is no clean line from the Union's transfers to a state road. Money arrives as a Treasury source that is in fact a loan, as an earmarked fund, as a counterpart, as a transfer. The page maps where the money is visible and where it is not.
- How to read it: five parts, as on the cover, each ending in the sentence that opens the next: roads are a state matter; the state pays with its own Treasury and with credit gated by the CAPAG, not with transfers; so the channels that matter are 1a and credit, and what mixes on the same work is the loan and its counterpart; credit pays the large works and the large works are the ones that extend and stall; hence the coding must separate size, channel and fiscal capacity. The macro series are in an appendix for questions. Colours are fixed per channel and per level of government on every chart.

## Part 5: Conclusions, the three answers (5 minutes)
1. More than one channel for the same work: yes for the works that matter. 21.5% of contracts, 47.0% of the paid value; a fifth to a third of the works where the work is identified; 40.2% of the federal highways carry two or more channels over the period.
2. Five problems, each on the whole valid base with its denominator: The contract is extended beyond its original term (states 49.8%, Union 50.4%); The contract value grows above the original value (states 47.6%, Union 43.9%); The contract is paid in a year of real fiscal cut (30% or more in the state's road payments) (states 33.6%, Union n/a); A large part of the payment is carried over to later years (restos a pagar above 25% of the paid value) (states 11.8%, Union 36.1%); The contract is interrupted (states: a year without payment between the first and the last; Union: a recorded stoppage or days stalled; two different measures, not comparable across spheres) (states 5.4%, Union 36.8%). The written reasons exist only in Santa Catarina and are an illustration, not a measure.
3. Successful cases as a funnel on the universe where every step is measurable (4,609 state contracts with term data, 2,234 federal): interruptions and rescissions remove few contracts; the extension is where the channels separate (Treasury 52.7% end within their term, domestic credit 24.7%, federal direct execution 5.6%). The federal drop at step 2 is a register status, to be validated. Size and channel are confounded; the coding must hold size constant.

## Three questions Vivien may ask (answers ready)
1. Where does the delay happen? In execution, not in payment or procurement. Payment is fast: in Minas Gerais the median note is paid one month after commitment (15,466 notes; 23% carried over); São Paulo pays 83% of the committed value in the year; signing to first payment is 3 months in the median where measured. Procurement is short where dated (Minas 4 months from process to validity; only SP, MG, RS have dates; the Union has none). Execution is long: 35.5 months from start order to current end in the federal contracts with payments (p90 72), 24 in the DNIT register; half of the contracts are extended in both spheres (states 49.8% of 5,198, Union 50.4%), the value grows above the original in 48% and 44%. What drives it: size and channel (Chart 20: Treasury small third 46% extended, large third 53%; fund 6% to 69%; credit-paid contracts end within term 25% against 53% for Treasury, Chart 21) and work type (Table 7: capacity expansion extended 64.7%, gap 10.1%, against maintenance 54.4% and 4.6%; federal construction 68.9% extended and 12.5% stalled or unfinished, recovery 24.9% and none). Fiscal shocks show as payment gaps: 35% of the gaps start in a year of real cut. The example: DER 18139/2012 (Chart 15).
2. What is the coverage of the base that links contracts? Ten states with a note-to-contract key: 12,910 contracts, R$ 101.0 bn, 62% of the R$ 161.8 bn these states paid on roads in 2010-2025 (Chart A8, Table 4). By state, paid-weighted: Piauí and Bahia 99%, Rio Grande do Sul 91%, Santa Catarina 80%, Minas 79%, Goiás 78%, Rio 58%, Pernambuco 52%, São Paulo 48%, Paraná 39%. Four states of the base (CE, MT, MS, PA) have no key and enter only the spending tables. São Paulo before 2022: 23% of the paid went to DERSA (own contracts, outside the DER register), 11% to expropriations, 31% to builders with more than one possible contract and no number on the note; from 2022 the link is 69% to 96%. Union: 3,274 contracts with payments (R$ 39.8 bn) but the note link exists from 2020 (2% to 16% before, 82% to 92% from 2021 with ObrasGov); the DNIT register (7,516 contracts) covers 2001-2025 without payments. Say it as a result: traceability is itself a governance measure, uneven across the federation, and it is what OC4IDS and PEFA PI-24 ask for.
3. Why do multichannels matter? They are the rule in the works that matter. 21.5% of state contracts are paid by more than one channel but they carry 47.0% of the paid value (Chart 10); one family alone pays 48.6% of the value; Treasury plus credit is 15.4% (loan and counterpart on the same contract), Treasury plus Union 9.1%. By state, the share of value in mixed contracts: Minas 69%, Bahia 68%, Goiás 61%, Piauí 51%, Pernambuco 49%. On the federal asset, 40% of the panel's highways received two or more channels over the period. Against single-channel contracts (Table 6) the multichannel contract is larger (6.9 against 2.1 mn), longer (28 against 12 months), extended more (68.2% against 45.5%), grows in value more (70.6% against 39.8%) and is interrupted more (11.3% against 5.5%), and the gap survives within size terciles and within maintenance; among large capacity-expansion works the two groups are alike, so there the work explains the incident, not the mix; only carry-over runs the other way (6.8% against 10.2%). By object the gap holds in every class (capacity expansion 72.4% against 59.4% extended; maintenance 73.8% against 47.5%), but object and size together show where it bites: among the large capacity-expansion works the two groups are alike (75.7% against 78.5%), so there the large work explains the incident; among the large maintenance contracts they are not (77.9% against 52.7% extended, 36 against 12 months): the multichannel maintenance contract is the long loan-financed programme contract with its counterpart, renewed and repriced along the way. Are the large works the multichannel ones? Yes: the share of multichannel contracts rises from 1.4% in the bottom decile of paid value to 56.3% in the top decile and 69.0% among the 100 largest; it rises with the length of the stretch too (18.2% to 45.1%). And the state matters as much as the size: in the large third Bahia and Minas mix channels in 64.5% and 69.5% of the contracts, São Paulo in 29.1% (DERSA and Treasury pay the large works) and Rio in 14.0% (sources unreadable). Unit: the contract, by its own notes; the road and work levels are in answer 1 and must not be mixed with it. Message for the coding: size, work type and state come before any channel effect. For governance each extra channel adds an auditor and a regime (TCU, lender rules, Senate): 18% of state contracts fall under two or more regimes (Table 5), and the mix is where tracing breaks (404 of 3,224 credit-paid contracts name the loan on the note). The case: the Rodoanel Norte, four channels, three executors, no ledger with the chain (Charts 5 and A5). On the federal side the mix lives on the asset, not on the contract: only 10 old contracts (2002-2005) carry a loan tranche; the large duplication of the BR-116/BA (prototype) is single-channel on its notes, while the highway carries DNIT, the ViaBahia concession and the state's access-road contracts.
- Caveat to say first: problem 5 (interruption) is 5.4% for the states and 36.8% for the Union because the measures differ (a year without payment against a recorded stoppage); it is not a performance gap.

## If she asks whether the channel matters beyond description (2 minutes)
- The causal audit (https://claude.ai/artifact/L7kfmCn64yu1bw3aZbJEaY, section 9) compares the contract with itself: the same contract, the same year, one note paid from the loan and one from the Treasury's regular source. The loan note is carried over 16.5 pp less in São Paulo (t 5.3, 127 contracts, 2010-2016) and 36 pp less in Goiás with the commitment month held constant (t 3.0); pooled 22.6 pp, wild bootstrap by contract p = 0.001; leaving any one contract out never changes the sign. Minas and Rio Grande do Sul stay out for lack of outcome data, not for their result.
- The federal notes point the same way (free Treasury notes carry over 38%, earmarked sources less) but have no commitment date, so the source cannot be separated from the end-of-year timing: suggestive. Next data step: the date of every note from the Portal da Transparência.
- What closed: the fiscal-shock design has five shock state-years and no signal; concessions do not change deaths per km once the controls are comparable, and the fall in deaths per crash rests on one cohort.
- One sentence: inside the same contract, the channel changes the payment stage; this is demonstrated in two states with every robustness test, and it is the empirical core the governance coding builds on.
