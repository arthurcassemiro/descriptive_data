# Presentation script: road financing in Brazil, described
Meeting with Vivien, 22 October 2026. Page: descriptive_statistics_road_financing.html. Metadata: metadata_descriptive_statistics.xlsx (one sheet per chart). Time: about 35 minutes plus discussion.

## Opening (2 minutes)
- What this is: the descriptive layer of the thesis, built so that I can start coding governance parameters. Every number comes from the bases we have been consolidating; nothing is typed by hand; stages are never added; what cannot be measured is said.
- The question behind the page: there is no clean line from the Union's transfers to a state road. Money arrives as a Treasury source that is in fact a loan, as an earmarked fund, as a counterpart, as a transfer. The page maps where the money is visible and where it is not.
- How to read it: five parts, as on the cover: the investment picture, the federative side of the states, the five channels, the contracts, and the conclusions with three answers. Colours are fixed per channel and per level of government on every chart.

## Part 1: Infrastructure General Data (5 minutes)
- Chart 1. General government investment peaked at 2.8% of GDP in 2010 and fell to 1.3% in 2017; states and municipalities invest more than the central government in every year. The thesis is about the subnational layer for a reason.
- Chart 2. Transport is 69.7% of the Union's infrastructure investment since 2010. PAC peak in 2009-2011, 2015 fall, recovery from 2023. State data by function only start in 2019 in Panel 1: say so.
- Chart 3. On the one classification that compares levels (Tesouro COFOG, 2010-2024), the states invest more in transport than the Union every year: 2024 states 42.0 bn, Union 11.9 bn, municipalities 40.2 bn.

## Part 2: Federative Aspects of the States (6 minutes)
- Chart 4a. State credit comes in waves: 2009-2014, trough 2018-2020, new wave from 2022, now domestic. Two sources (STN accounts to 2012, DCA from 2013), break declared.
- Chart 4b and 4c. External loans concentrate on states in 2008-2014; the transport-specific universe shows the same wave, mostly BNDES and Caixa in 2012-2014.
- Chart 5. The states' debt is debt with the Union: 66.7% of the consolidated debt in 2024 is the restructured debt of Law 9.496; loans with lenders are 21.8%. Net debt over RCL fell from 170.2% in 2000 to 77.3% in 2025.
- Chart 6. ICMS is 41.0% of state revenue, FPE 11.5%, credit 2.7%, capital transfers 0.5%. Investment transfers are a rounding error in a state budget: first reason they are hard to trace.
- Chart 7. The Union's investment transfers to states: 17.7 bn in 2010, 6.5 bn in 2025; the transport part collapses after 2012 to under 0.2 bn a year. Federal road money reaches the states' territory through DNIT (1a federal), not through 1b.
- Table 1. CAPAG: MG, RJ and RS stuck at C or D; BA, CE, MT, PR, SC climb to A. This is the gate for guaranteed borrowing.
- Chart 8. Credit and transfers swing by tens of percent a year; revenue and debt move slowly. The road programmes sit on the volatile pair.

## Part 3: The Financing Channels Trajectories (9 minutes)
- Legend first: 1a own budget (federal through DNIT; state through Treasury or earmarked fund), 1b Union transfer, 2 state company, 3 domestic loan, 4 external loan, 5 private capital.
- Chart 9. Union: 1a carries the programme; 1b fades after 2012; 4 is a 2001-2006 feature; 5 appears in 2010 and grows with the 2024-2025 rounds. States: credit (3, 3/4, 4) paid 44.2% of road spending in 2013-2017 and 15.8% in 2021-2025; the grey band is spending with no readable source code (RJ, PR, RS).
- Chart 10 and 11. Roads are 0.2% to 0.7% of GDP and about a fifth to a quarter of general-government investment; procyclical and amplified (minus 40% in 2015, plus 40 to 50% in 2021-2022).
- Chart 12. What is built: capacity expansion is 29.0% of state paid and 12.5% of federal paid; the Union's contracts are mostly maintenance.
- Chart 13. 1a federal by source: free Treasury resources pay 71.6% since 2015. Flag P-056: the pre-2015 grouping of earmarked sources in Panel 1 needs a check.
- Chart 14. 1a state by source: Treasury carries it; earmarked funds reach 23.6% in 2023-2025; CIDE shrinks. By state: SP free money; PI, PE, MG earmarked; RJ, PR, RS unreadable.
- Table 2. Credit matters most in small and medium states; SP is the one large external borrower; only 404 of 3,224 credit-paid contracts name the loan on the note. This is the second reason the chain is hard to trace.
- Chart 15 and 16. Federal concessions: 34 contracts in force in 2025, capacity obligations 12.9 thousand km by 2026; BNDES lending to concessionaires is an attribute of channel 5 (borrower is private). São Paulo's own programme: 29 lots since 1998, 25 in force, about 7,407 km; listed concessionaires invested R$ 21.0 bn (2025 prices) in 2021-2025; the state pays PPP contributions (Tamoios, Rodoanel Norte).
- Table 3. 52 IDB and World Bank road loans to states, US$ 11,160 mn; 12 are multi-sector or rural with a road component; extensions and cancellations where measured.

## Part 4: Contracts Performance (11 minutes)
- Table 4. Three layers, three windows: 12,910 state contracts (10 states, R$ 101.0 bn, 2010-2025), 3,274 federal contracts with payments (R$ 39.8 bn, payments from 2020), DNIT register 7,516. CE, MT, MS, PA have no note-to-contract key.
- Chart 17. Coverage of the link by state and year; the Union row is a different measure (share of paid whose action names the highway, 94% in 2010 to about a quarter today).
- Chart 18. Composition of the contract: one family pays 48.6% of the value, two or more pay 47.1%; Treasury plus credit alone is 15.4%.
- Charts 19 to 21. Duration: half of state contracts end within a year, half of federal contracts last more than three years; federal capacity expansion median 38 months; by channel, credit-paid contracts last longer than Treasury ones (3: 23, 4: 17, 1a state: 13 months).
- Chart 22 and 23. Stages: procurement is visible in four states (modality) and three (date or year); the slow stage is execution (federal start order to end, 35 months median), not payment (MG pays within a month in the median; SP pays 83% in the year). Carry-over is the payment-stage signal: 14% of paid in the median federal contract, 37% at p75. By channel in MG, fund notes are carried over twice as often as Treasury notes.
- Chart 24 and 25. Rescissions: only MG and the DNIT register publish the category; year is the current end date (proxy, declared). MG rescinds in waves; fund and CIDE contracts are rescinded more than Treasury ones.
- Finding 1. Cuts cluster in 2015, 2018-2019 and 2023; the Union's real road spending falls 40% in 2015 and recovers only in 2023. Timeline, not causality.
- Finding 2. Cuts are more frequent where debt over RCL is above the median and where CAPAG is C or D; payment gaps follow the same order. Small n, association only.
- Chart 26. Extension by size and channel: larger contracts are extended more often in every channel, and the extended ones are extended for longer; 1a federal and 1a state apart. The scatter is folded under the chart for whoever wants the points.
- Table 5. Who audits: transfers and loans add auditors (TCU, lender, Senate); 18% of state contracts fall under two or more regimes.
- Finding 3. The delay categories for the coding: fiscal, financing, procurement, execution, payment, each with its indicator and coverage.

## Part 5: Conclusions, the three answers (5 minutes)
1. More than one channel for the same work: yes for the works that matter. 21.5% of contracts, 47.0% of the paid value; a fifth to a third of the works where the work is identified; 40.2% of the federal highways carry two or more channels over the period.
2. Five reasons, ranked by share of contracts within coverage: Term extension of the contract (any) (49.8%); Design change (project revised during the works) (45.1%); Payment gap on credit-paid contracts (a year without payment) (8.7%); Budget or financial restriction (gaps that start in cut years; amendment motive) (35.6%); Stoppage, days stalled and unfinished works (36.8%). Motives are readable only where the register publishes the amendment text or the stoppage.
3. Successful cases: the clean finish is the small and medium Treasury-paid contract; large works, credit-paid or federal, extend, pause and are rescinded more. Size and channel are confounded; the coding must hold size constant.

## Closing (2 minutes)
- What this enables: the governance codebook can now be written on measured fields (channel, earmark, auditor, stage times, extension, gap, rescission) with explicit coverage per state.
- What stays open: four states without a contract key; the loan named on the note in only 404 of 3,224 credit contracts; no rescission dates; tender dates in three states (SP, MG, RS), modality in four; P-056 on Panel 1's pre-2015 source grouping.
- Ask: whether the five delay categories and the strict success definition are the right ones to carry into the coding, and whether Part 2 (the federative side) stays in the body of the thesis chapter or moves to an appendix.
